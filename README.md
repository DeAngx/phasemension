# phasemension

**phase + dimension**

Exploratory space for programmable interfaces, density shifts, faceted non-equilibrium states, and multi-axis control of form.

Bubbles that don't pop.  
Crystals that still remember being soft.  
The harness that keeps a configuration real after the usual supporting conditions are withdrawn.

---

## What this is

A sandbox for thinking and coding about systems where:

- **surface** is not just a boundary but a controllable object
- **density** can be driven and held away from its ambient preference
- **topology / organization** can be selected rather than only relaxed into
- multiple axes (energy, geometry, information, constraint) act at once

Ordinary phase change is one-axis: heat water → it boils.  
Here we are interested in what happens when the material (or the configuration) is addressed from several directions simultaneously — so that the transition stops looking like a forced ladder and starts looking like a choice of topology or density.

## Core questions being played with

1. What does it take to keep a bubble from popping — not by delaying collapse, but by continuously re-supplying the constraints that define its existence?
2. What does a programmable interface look like when density and persistence conditions are first-class?
3. How do you go from a sustained non-equilibrium membrane to a faceted crystalline form without losing the memory of the earlier state?
4. What language lets you talk about *allowed existence* the way current languages talk about temperature or voltage?

## Starting directions

- Phase-field / level-set methods with anisotropic terms (faceting)
- Non-equilibrium steady states and dissipative structures
- Multi-axis constraint systems (the harness)
- Discrete differential geometry on evolving meshes
- Anything that treats "does not collapse" as an active, maintained property
