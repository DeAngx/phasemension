# Opening Notes

## The non-popping bubble

A soap bubble is a temporary self-organized surface under tension.  
Squeeze past threshold → topology fails → "gone".

From outside: disappeared.  
From inside the event: the organizing constraint was withdrawn and the system fell back to ambient density.

A harness that keeps it from popping is not just resistance.  
It continuously re-supplies the missing constraints from multiple directions at once:
- surface tension / curvature energy
- local field or vacuum condition
- information density of the boundary
- whatever is actually load-bearing for that particular configuration

The bubble no longer lives on borrowed time. It can be parked.

That is already a micro-version of keeping a density.

## From bubble to crystal-like ball

The same multi-axis system that was preventing collapse can be retuned.  
Surface energy becomes anisotropic.  
The membrane stops being a tense hollow skin and starts preferring flat faces and edges.  
What was sustained non-equilibrium membrane becomes ordered solid.

Question worth keeping: does the crystal keep any memory of having been the bubble?  
Or does the new organization overwrite the previous one completely?

## Axes

1 axis ≈ forced transition along a line (temperature, pressure, …)  
2 axes ≈ surfaces + simple conjunction/disjunction (first real logic-gate territory)  
3+ axes ≈ higher-dimensional control volumes where the material can be addressed from enough directions that the transition stops looking like phase change and starts looking like a choice of configuration.

The number of axes matters less than whether those axes can talk to each other.

## Working vocabulary so far

- **harness** — continuous multi-axis constraint that maintains non-default density or topology
- **parked state** — configuration held away from ambient equilibrium without continuous collapse pressure
- **faceted commitment** — when a soft interface chooses ordered geometry
- **density shift** — treating density itself as driven rather than passive
- **allowed existence** — the semantic layer that decides whether a configuration is permitted to persist once usual supports are removed
