# High-pressure ice / water phase boundaries — terrestrial forcing (Phase B draft)

Process chosen: high-pressure solid phases of H₂O (Ice VI, VII, X and related) and their boundaries with liquid water under planetary interior conditions.

Goal: force the locked phasemension vocabulary against a real Earth system without invention. Record where the language fits, where it is silent, and where it breaks.

---

## 1. Process in ordinary geophysical terms

Water under increasing pressure does not stay ordinary ice or liquid. Above ~0.6 GPa, denser crystalline phases appear:

- **Ice VI** — tetragonal, forms from liquid near ~1.1 GPa; density ~1.31 g/cm³.
- **Ice VII** — cubic, proton-disordered; stable above ~2.2 GPa; density ~1.65 g/cm³. Observed as inclusions in natural diamonds sourced from the mantle transition zone (~410–660 km), with residual pressures of several to ~24 GPa.
- **Ice X** — denser, protons centered between oxygens; enters at tens of GPa.
- At still higher T/P, **superionic** regimes (mobile protons, fixed oxygen lattice) appear in models and experiments relevant to deep planetary interiors.

In Earth, free bulk Ice VII is not a permanent global layer the way it can be in thick hydrospheres of icy moons or ocean worlds. Evidence for deep aqueous fluid (and its frozen residue as Ice VII in diamonds) points to localized, water-rich regions, especially in the transition zone and cold subduction pathways. Density of any H₂O parcel is set by the local P–T path and composition. Phase boundaries move when P or T change; latent heat and ΔV accompany the transitions.

Energy account for a parcel crossing a boundary is classical: latent heat (enthalpy of transition) + PV work associated with volume change. Clapeyron slope links dP/dT to ΔV and ΔH.

## 2. Forced mapping of locked terms

### Density as driven

**Fits.** Density is driven by pressure, temperature, and phase identity. Ice VII is denser than Ice VI; both are denser than liquid water at the same conditions. There is no free choice of density independent of the local state. A parcel’s density follows the equilibrium (or metastable) phase diagram under the ambient P–T field.

**Silent / break.** The locked phrase “density held away from ambient preference” does not map cleanly. In this system, sustained density is *local equilibrium under planetary boundary conditions*, not a non-equilibrium density maintained against the surroundings by continuous external constraint. What looks “held” is simply stable at that depth.

### Observation vs intervention

**Fits as pure observation.** Natural evolution of these phases is observation-only: no external agent is applying `do(P)` or `do(T)`. Energy of a closed parcel is conserved except for heat/work exchange with the surrounding rock/fluid already accounted as boundary fluxes.

**Intervention (hypothetical).** An intervention would be an external change that forces a variable off the natural path — e.g. a localized heat pulse, a sudden stress change, or artificial compression. Then ΔE must be logged: heat added, or work ∫P dV (or equivalent). The Phase A audit rule applies without modification: silent state overrides are invalid.

**Fits.** The observation/intervention boundary is clear and does not need invention.

### Harness

**Mostly breaks or stays silent.**

A harness was defined as continuous multi-axis re-supply of constraints that keep a configuration from collapsing to ambient preference. In high-pressure ice, the “constraints” are the planetary pressure and temperature fields themselves. They are not an active, local, continuous intervention by an agent; they are the background state of the planet.

There is no evidence here of a non-default density or phase being *maintained against* local equilibrium by ongoing energy throughput in the harness sense. Metastability can exist (e.g. residual pressure in diamond inclusions), but that is mechanical confinement by the host, not a programmable or multi-axis control loop.

**Honest statement:** this process does not supply a natural example of a harness. It supplies examples of equilibrium (and metastable) density under extreme boundary conditions.

### Joule ledger

**Fits.** For a parcel crossing Ice VI ↔ liquid or Ice VII ↔ liquid (or solid–solid boundaries):

- Configurational term: internal energy / enthalpy difference between phases (latent heat).
- PV work from ΔV at the transition pressure.
- Kinetic term: usually negligible for slow geologic rates; declare ~0 for quasi-static paths.

Conservation audit is well-defined. Laboratory or simulation work that forces a transition must account for the energy injected or extracted. No special amendment to Phase A is required.

### Soft → ordered / faceted; memory

**Partial fit.** Liquid → Ice VII (or VI) is a disorder → order transition in the oxygen lattice (with proton disorder remaining in VII). Crystal structure is anisotropic in growth kinetics even when the equilibrium shape is cubic (VII). Whether any structural or impurity “memory” of the prior fluid state survives in the solid is a real materials question (trapped chemistry, defect populations, residual stress) and is not answered by the phase diagram alone.

The locked interest in memory across soft → ordered transitions is *not contradicted* and is *not demonstrated* by the mere existence of the phases. It remains an open empirical question for this system.

## 4. What this forcing does *not* claim

- That high-pressure ice is programmable matter.
- That a through-Earth tunnel is implied or supported.
- That planetary P–T fields are a harness in the project sense.
- That the locked frame is validated in full. Several load-bearing terms (harness, non-default density hold) do not find a natural home here.

---

## 5. Consequence for the frame

This process is a strong terrestrial example of **driven density and phase under extreme boundary conditions** with a clean energy account. It is a weak or null example of **harness** and of **density sustained against ambient preference**.

Phase B exit for *this* process: the write-up exists; fits and breaks are explicit. Further Phase B work can pick a second process (e.g. anisotropic crystal growth under stress, or melt migration) if a positive example of harness-like behavior is needed, or accept that harness remains an abstract / artificial construct not yet forced by Earth systems.
